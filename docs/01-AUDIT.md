# Audit — aydins-portfolio.webflow.io
_Read on 23 Aug 2026. Based on the full public site: home + 8 project pages, 61 images._

## The one-line read

The work is genuinely senior. The site presents it as junior.

That gap is the entire problem, and it's fixable without shooting anything new.

---

## What's strong (keep and build on)

**1. Balkan Transfer is a real senior case study.** End-to-end ownership: research → IA → wireframes → UI → spec → handoff, across web *and* mobile, with a named team you led. The UI sheet shows the full booking funnel — auth, search, map picker, passenger config, extras, checkout, payment, post-booking management. That's not a screen exercise, that's a product.

**2. This sentence is the best writing on the site:**
> We reframed the experience from "fill forms → submit" to "resolve decisions → commit"

That's the voice for the whole portfolio. It's a strategic reframe stated in nine words. Everything else should be rewritten to match this register.

**3. Cuckoo is underrated.** Seven discrete product decisions, each with problem → constraint → decision → outcome. The completion-badge one is the standout: *"challenge avatars sometimes include sponsor logos → cannot obscure them."* Designing around a business constraint and saying so is a senior tell. Most portfolios hide constraints; showing them is what separates you.

**4. Innos.** Dense, information-heavy B2B UI executed cleanly, in three days, under a deliberately vague brief. "The Head of Design said it was the best submission they'd received" is real external validation.

**5. Asset quality is high.** 2000–3000px source images, consistent 1962×992 cover treatment. Nothing needs re-exporting.

---

## What's holding it back

### The positioning doesn't match your ask
You told me you're a **Product Designer + Design Engineer**. The site says "Senior Product Designer" and contains **zero evidence of the engineering half**. No code, no live components, no build artifact — and the site itself is a Webflow page.

For a design engineer, the portfolio *is* the primary work sample. Right now yours argues against the title.

### "Available on request" appears twice
> "Full case studies & recent work are available on request"
> "Full case studies and detailed walkthroughs are available upon request."

A hiring manager gives you 90 seconds. This tells them the good stuff isn't here and asks them to do work to see it. Nobody writes that email. If material is under NDA, show what you *can* — flows, systems thinking, redacted screens, a written decision log — but never gate the homepage.

### The biggest story is buried
Pickleball.com — **6 interconnected products, component libraries, typography, cross-product brand systems, since Feb 2024** — is your most senior work and it appears only as one clause in an About paragraph. Two-and-a-half years of design-systems work at product-family scale, with no case study.

That's the single largest gap on the site.

### Voice slips into junior register
Direct quotes:
- "I am proud of the work that we did on this project." (Akbank — says nothing about the work)
- "these are my first steps as a professional designer :)" (Morpa Kampüs)
- "Hope you enjoy to look at the screens as much as I enjoy while I build them"
- "unfortunately I do not have the user research part anymore"
- "While not all AI-driven product suggestions are currently adopted due to company priorities" — an apology placed inside your pitch. Never explain that your ideas weren't adopted.
- She Ventures reads as *"I asked Google Bard and it liked my design"* — AI as novelty, not as leverage.

### Typos in body copy
`plartform`, `puslish`, `secreen`, `moile`, `Pyschology`, `childrens do`. On a design portfolio, these read as carelessness — the trait you least want attributed to you.

### The Morpa Ders page is broken
"About the project" is duplicated **three times** verbatim, and the Morpa Kampüs write-up is pasted into it. Anyone who scrolls sees it immediately.

### No outcomes anywhere
Everything is "expected qualitative impact." Not one number, not one before/after, not one shipped-and-measured result. Senior portfolios don't need a dashboard — but they need *something* falsifiable.
